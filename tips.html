01 · AMIGURUMI TIPS

Tiny stitches, tidy toys

Amigurumi is easier when each little detail has a job. A smooth, light-colored yarn helps you see your stitches, while a hook smaller than the label's usual suggestion creates a dense fabric that holds stuffing inside.

  • Mark the first stitch of every round and count at the end of each one.
  • Use invisible decreases to help keep the fabric smooth.
  • Stuff in small amounts; stop before the stitches stretch apart.
  • Pin ears, arms, or facial features in place before sewing them down.
  • For babies and pets, embroider eyes and avoid detachable parts.
02 · CROCHET TIPS & TRICKS

Make every row a little easier

Even experienced crocheters return to the basics. Before starting a big project, create a small gauge swatch and compare it with your pattern. A consistent stitch height is often more important than trying to crochet quickly.

  • Use the same hook and yarn tension throughout your project.
  • Keep a small notebook for hook sizes, gauge, and pattern adjustments.
  • Count turning chains correctly: a pattern will tell you whether they count as a stitch.
  • Pull out a few stitches to correct an early mistake rather than hiding it later.
  • Weave in ends in several directions for a more secure finish.
03 · YARN GUIDE

Yarn fibers, weights & what they're for

Two things matter most when choosing yarn: what it's made of and how thick it is.

Cotton offers crisp stitch definition and is handy for baskets, amigurumi, and summer accessories. Acrylic is affordable, colorful, and often easy to care for, making it a popular choice for blankets and toys. Wool brings warmth and elasticity for hats, sweaters, and winter pieces. Bamboo, linen, and blends can offer drape or breathability for garments and shawls. Always follow the yarn's care label.

Yarn weights are standardized categories, not strict measurements. The table is a starting guide; check the specific yarn label and your pattern for hook size and gauge.

Weight Common name Popular uses Typical hook*
0 Lace Lace, crochet thread Fine lace, delicate shawls, doilies 1.5–2.25 mm
1 Super Fine Fingering, sock Light shawls, baby items, fine wearables 2.25–3.5 mm
2 Fine Sport, baby Light sweaters, toys, soft baby blankets 3.5–4.5 mm
3 Light DK, light worsted Garments, hats, soft toys, blankets 4.5–5.5 mm
4 Medium Worsted, aran Amigurumi, scarves, throws, beginner projects 5.5–6.5 mm
5 Bulky Chunky Quick hats, cozy scarves, pillows 6.5–9 mm
6 Super Bulky Super chunky Fast blankets, plush accessories, baskets 9–15 mm
7 Jumbo Jumbo, roving-style Very chunky blankets and statement decor 15 mm and up

*Approximate crochet-hook ranges for general guidance, not universal standards. For amigurumi, a smaller hook is often used to form a firm fabric.
