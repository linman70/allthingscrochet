STUFFED WITH IMAGINATION

Three things to love about amigurumi

01 / THE BASICS

What is amigurumi?

Amigurumi is the Japanese art of making small stuffed figures using crochet or knitting. Modern crocheted amigurumi often uses single crochet worked in continuous spirals, which creates a firm fabric ideal for shaping bears, bunnies, fruit, and fantasy creatures.

The secret is surprisingly simple: increases make a circle grow, straight rounds add height, and decreases close a shape. Add stuffing and an embroidered expression, and your little character comes to life.

Start with a round character that has very few pieces to sew together.
02 / MATERIALS

Choosing the right supplies

Smooth cotton or acrylic yarn makes stitches easier to see and count. Worsted-weight yarn with a hook a little smaller than the yarn label suggests is often a friendly starting point. A stitch marker keeps track of round beginnings while a tapestry needle helps join pieces neatly.

Stuff slowly with small tufts of polyester fill to avoid lumpy shapes. For toys intended for babies or pets, embroider eyes and skip safety eyes, buttons, bells, beads, and any other detachable parts.

A tight fabric keeps stuffing inside and gives your figure a clean shape.
03 / PERSONALITY

Shape, color & expression

Amigurumi is a playful lesson in construction. Start with a sphere or oval, build ears or limbs separately, and pin the parts in place before stitching them on. Small changes in the eyes, mouth, and placement of the ears can completely change a toy's personality.

Try a limited color palette, count stitches at the end of each round, and use invisible decreases when working through both loops. After your first figure, change the colors or give it tiny accessories to make it yours.

Make both limbs before attaching them so you can compare their sizes.
FREE BEGINNER PATTERN

Sunny Little Tangerine 🍊

A cheerful, palm-size amigurumi fruit, worked in continuous rounds.

Beginner US terms Approx. 3 in / 7.5 cm About 1–2 hours

You'll need

  • Orange worsted-weight yarn, about 30–45 yd
  • Small amount of green yarn for a leaf and stem
  • 3.5 mm (E/4) crochet hook or size giving a firm fabric
  • Polyester stuffing, stitch marker, tapestry needle, scissors
  • Black embroidery floss for optional sleepy eyes

Stitch key

MR magic ring · sc single crochet · inc 2 sc in one stitch · dec invisible decrease · sl st slip stitch · ch chain. Numbers in parentheses are the stitch count at the end of the round.

Body — orange yarn

  1. R1: 6 sc into MR. (6)
  2. R2: Inc in each stitch around. (12)
  3. R3: [1 sc, inc] × 6. (18)
  4. R4: [2 sc, inc] × 6. (24)
  5. R5: [3 sc, inc] × 6. (30)
  6. R6: [4 sc, inc] × 6. (36)
  7. R7–R12: Sc around for 6 rounds. (36 each)
  8. R13: [4 sc, dec] × 6. (30)
  9. R14: [3 sc, dec] × 6. (24)
  10. R15: [2 sc, dec] × 6. (18) Stuff firmly but gently.
  11. R16: [1 sc, dec] × 6. (12)
  12. R17: Dec × 6. (6) Fasten off, thread through front loops of remaining stitches and pull closed.

Leaf & finishing

  1. Leaf: With green yarn, ch 6. In the second ch from hook, sl st, then sc, hdc, sc, sl st along the chain. Fasten off, leaving a tail.
  2. Stem: Ch 4 with green yarn, sl st back along the chain. Sew the stem and leaf over the body's closing point.
  3. Optional face: Embroider two small curved eyes before final stuffing, keeping them on the same round.
  4. Gently shape the fruit with your hands and weave in all ends securely.

Original beginner pattern by All Things Crochet. The freely licensed fruit photographs show a similar crocheted tangerine made by another artist; yours may look different.

✿ MAKER TIP

Use a removable stitch marker in the first stitch of each new round. Move it up as you go so your stitch counts stay on track.
