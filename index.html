A little history of crochet

The word crochet comes from the French word for “small hook.” While looped textile traditions go back much further, crochet in its recognizable modern form became widely documented during the 1800s. Pattern books and magazines helped popularize delicate lace, home linens, and clothing, especially across Europe.

Over time, makers experimented with new fibers, colors, and shapes. Today, the craft spans everything from heirloom shawls to colorful amigurumi characters.

Why crocheting feels so good

Crochet gives your hands something creative to do and turns a simple ball of yarn into a one-of-a-kind gift. Repeating stitches can feel soothing, and a finished project brings a satisfying sense of accomplishment.

It's also flexible: start with a ten-minute mini project, customize almost any color, reuse leftover yarn, or share what you make with friends and community groups.
